# Content

Content is how a small team reaches many people without talking to each one. It pays slowly and keeps paying, so it suits strategies chosen for compounding rather than speed (`gtm/choose-strategies`). It only works if each piece has a reader, a point and sourced claims.

## Start from a brief

Write a short brief before drafting, for a person or an agent. One page at most:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Reader | who this is for, from a positioning block, and what they are trying to do |
| Question | the single question or problem the piece answers |
| Takeaway | what the reader should be able to do or decide afterwards |
| Format and length | post, guide, thread, script, clip; rough size |
| Evidence | the fact rows and any demos, screenshots or data to use |
| Call to action | one next step, with its tracked link |
| Campaign | which campaign and goal this serves (`gtm/campaigns`) |
| Out of scope | what not to cover or claim |

A piece without a reader and a takeaway is not ready to write.

## Long-form (blogs, guides, docs-style posts)

- Lead with the problem in the reader's words, then the answer; do not make them wait through your history.
- Prefer pieces only you can write: something you built, measured or learned. A first-hand account beats a summary of other posts.
- Show real examples, commands and screenshots that work. Run anything you publish.
- Teach enough to be useful without the product, then show where the product helps. A guide that is only a pitch gets ignored.
- Structure for skimming: clear headings, short paragraphs, one idea each.
- Include the number, date and method for any figure, and link primary sources.
- Refresh or retire old posts that no longer match the product.

## Short-form (posts, threads, replies)

- Say one thing. Link to the long piece for depth instead of squeezing it in.
- Write for the platform's norms; a reply that helps someone beats a standalone promotional post.
- Do not reuse the same text across places with the same audience. Adapt it.
- In communities that restrict self-promotion, contribute first and follow the rules; if in doubt, ask a moderator.

## Video

- Decide the format by the job: a short demo showing the first result, a walkthrough of a task, a talk, or a founder explaining a decision.
- Open with what the viewer will get. Show the real product working, not a mock-up passed off as real.
- Script the key points and the claims; keep takes honest, and never imply a result you did not get on screen.
- Add captions and a transcript; they help accessibility and let people skim.

## Voice

Write the way a capable colleague would explain it: plain, specific and direct. Keep a short voice note in the repo (three to five rules and a few sentences you like) so different authors, and agents, sound alike. Things to avoid: hype adjectives, "revolutionary", "game-changing", filler openings, fake urgency, invented anecdotes and made-up statistics. If a sentence sounds like it could describe any product, rewrite it with something specific to yours.

## Claims check before publishing

Every piece passes this check, performed by someone other than the drafter when possible:

1. List every factual claim: numbers, comparisons, capabilities, customer names, quotes, "first/only/best".
2. Match each to a fact row (`gtm/positioning`). No row, no claim: remove it, soften it to what you can show, or add the verified source.
3. Check that numbers are current and that comparisons name the method and the competitor's current version.
4. Confirm permission is recorded for any customer name, logo or quote.
5. Run each command and open each link and screenshot as the reader would.
6. Check tone and rules: no unsupported superlatives, no promotion where it is forbidden, no personal data exposed in screenshots.

## Publish

**A human approves each piece before it is published or posted.** An agent can research, outline, draft and run the claims check, then queue the piece with its fact references; the human reads it and presses publish. Update the campaign table's status when it goes out, and record the URL and its tracked link.

## Rules

- No invented statistics, quotes, customers or benchmarks.
- No publishing, posting or sending without approval.
- Disclose affiliations and sponsorships when they apply.
- Credit sources and respect licences for images, code and data you did not make.
