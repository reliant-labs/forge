# Events

Events are a high-touch channel: slow and expensive per contact, but good for trust, feedback and partnerships. They suit products with a reachable, concentrated audience, and are a poor fit when your buyers are scattered and self-serve.

## Step 1: Decide if events fit your strategy

Check `gtm/choose-strategies` first. Events are worth the time when you can name the audience, the audience gathers in identifiable rooms, and your model rewards relationships (higher-priced, trust-heavy or integration-heavy products). If your first customers will find you through a search and a free tier, put the time elsewhere.

## Step 2: Find relevant events

Build a candidate list from several sources instead of the first search result:

- community calendars and meetup platforms for your audience's topic and region
- conference sites and call-for-proposals listings in your field
- hackathon listings, including those run by platforms your product integrates with
- where your customers and partners say they are going, in their posts and bios
- communities you already belong to, where members announce and recount events

For each candidate record: name, date, place or online, organiser, audience description, size, cost to attend and to speak or sponsor, deadlines, and the link. Finding events is recurring work and a good candidate for automation (`gtm/automate`, "Weekly event finder").

## Step 3: Judge audience fit

Score each event against your positioning before spending anything:

- **Audience match:** are the attendees the people in your audience block, in the roles that decide or influence? Read past agendas, speaker lists and attendee write-ups.
- **Density:** how many relevant people will you actually be able to talk to?
- **Format:** a talk, a booth, a workshop, a hallway track or a hackathon each suit different goals. A small meetup where you can have real conversations often beats a big expo hall.
- **Cost:** ticket, travel, time away, sponsorship. Include the hours you will not spend on the product.
- **Give-and-get:** can you offer something useful (a talk, a workshop, a prize, a demo) rather than only collecting contacts?
- **Reputation:** is the event run well, with a code of conduct? Do attendees say it was worth it?

Pick few. One or two well-prepared events a quarter beat a calendar full of half-attended ones.

## Step 4: Prepare

- Set one goal for the event: three conversations with people in the audience, one partner intro, a talk that earns ten signups. Write it down.
- Prepare a short, honest description of what you do, built from your positioning. Have a demo that works offline or on bad Wi-Fi.
- Decide what you will ask people, not only what you will tell them.
- Prepare a way to capture follow-ups with consent: a short form or a note of who agreed to hear from you. Do not scan badges or collect contacts in ways the event forbids.
- For a talk or workshop, pitch value to the audience first and the product second, and check every claim against the facts list.
- For a hackathon, make the starter materials and a named contact easy to find, and be present to help.
- Pack a plain handout or link, not a stack of swag.

## Step 5: Show up usefully

Listen more than you pitch. Ask what people are working on, take notes right after each conversation, and offer help that costs you little. Stay for the unscheduled parts, where most of the value is.

## Step 6: Follow up

Within a couple of days, send a short, specific note to each person who agreed to hear from you, referring to what you actually talked about. Do not add anyone to a newsletter or sequence they did not agree to. Log outcomes against the goal, and record what you would change.

## Rules

- A human approves any registration, sponsorship, travel booking, speaking submission or follow-up message. An agent or script may find, score and draft; it does not commit money or contact people.
- No collecting or messaging attendees against the event's rules or the law (see the opt-out notes in `gtm/outreach`).
- Do not claim sponsorship, partnership or endorsement by an event or organiser unless it is in the facts list.

## When this skill is not enough

- Automating the weekly search and shortlist: `gtm/automate`.
- Writing the follow-ups and the talk's abstract: `gtm/outreach`, `gtm/content`.
- Measuring whether events paid off: `gtm/measure`.
